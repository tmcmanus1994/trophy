      ### The reset rules (post-DLC — this changed)
      - **Suspend/Resume no longer heals you.** Before the free Muzan DLC,

      ### Plan A — Fortified Grab Loop (Tanjiro, Water Breathing)
      Gear: **[Enhanced Throw]** (grab damage scales up to 4x on repeat grabs)
      + **[Fortified Equipment]** (stat boost after activating any gear 3 times).
      Opener, dashing in with ○:
      `□ □ □ → △ → □ □ □ → △ → (↑ + △) → R1+□`
      The sequence is built so the **grab lands last**, at max scaling. Then:
      wait ~1 second after the grab animation, dash in so you're standing on
      them as they wake, and grab again — **grabs beat blocking**, and a woken
      CPU's instinct is to block. That's the loop: block → grab → knockdown.
      **The loop is not flawless — the CPU sometimes wakes up swinging.**
      When it does, don't fight it out: reset the round.
      *Defensive variant:* let them open, dodge/escape with the evade, then
      run the same combo. Holding block as you close also baits the CPU into
      blocking, which is exactly what you want.

      ### Plan B — Akaza zoning (Compass Needle)
      Gear: **[Timely Reversal]** (special gauge fills faster while you're
      behind on HP — i.e. exactly when you need it) + **[Skill 3 Power Up]**
      (Skill 3 damage grows after 3 uses, **and carries into the next round**;
      it resets when you get hit, and does not trigger during Surge).
      Akaza's Skill 3 "Compass Needle" drops a snowflake zone; anything that
      steps in gets auto-attacked, and **the only counterplay is a perfectly
      timed guard**. Akaza is a Demon, so Boosting also **regenerates HP** —
      bank the special gauge and spend it as healing in the deep rounds.
      Plan B is the better pick if you're bad at wake-up timing; Plan A is
      better if you're bad at spacing.

      ### What S rank actually keys on
      There's no published numeric formula, but the weighting is consistent:
      1. **HP retained is the dominant term.** Taking little damage matters
         more than anything else you do.
      2. **Dodging enemy Ultimates and skills** specifically counts — eating a
         super is a double hit (damage + evasion score).
      3. Offensive output — chase dashes, support-character calls, Ultimate Arts.
      4. **Team/Duo Ultimates** (fill the red bar twice) noticeably push the
         rank up. Battles 2 and 6 have a partner, so these are available there.

      ### How to fight Advanced AI
      Advanced AI is relentless and will not respect a whiffed opener.
      **Watch their Skill Gauge, not their health** — that meter tells you when
      a super is coming and when it's safe to commit. Let them throw the first
      attack, dodge/escape it, then run your punish combo. The Fortified Grab
      Loop works here too and is the reason this trophy is much easier than
      its reputation: **grabs bypass block**, and Advanced AI blocks a lot.

      **The trap:** trying to S-rank by dealing huge damage fast. You'll trade
      hits, lose the HP term, and finish at A with the fight won. Slow, safe,
      untouched beats fast and bloody every time. **Battle 6 (Rengoku vs Akaza)
      is the one people re-run most** — Akaza is aggressive and punishes greed.

Endgame pickup: 35 of 38 done, and the three left are the platinum and the two
that actually hurt. Everything before this — story, S ranks, Training Paths,
the whole Kimetsu Points shop — is finished, so this run is nothing but
`Trials Prevailed` and `To the Death`. Do them in that order: the six Advanced
fights in The Path of a Demon Slayer are the training montage for Survival,
using the same gear and the same grab-loop timing on the same hyper-aggressive
AI, just without 100 rounds of accumulated risk riding on every exchange. Then
set Auto-Sync to Disabled, learn the Close-Game reset until it's reflex, and
grind the ladder — the roster loops every 46 fights, so by round 47 there's
nothing left to be surprised by except the damage numbers.
